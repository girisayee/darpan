# Naming And Branding Council

Date: 2026-06-20

User signal: `RealizedEdge` and `PositionIQ` are both rejected. Treat both as dead names for future UI exploration.

## Product Truth

This app is a personal trader cockpit for reviewing what actually happened after trades close.

It is:

- Local-first and private.
- Built from imported brokerage activity, currently Robinhood-style CSV data.
- Focused on realized P&L, option income, swing trades, capital efficiency, goal pace, tax lots, and review/fix workflows.
- Useful for eager retail traders who want to know what worked, what hurt, what needs attention, and whether they are on monthly/yearly pace.

It is not:

- A brokerage.
- A live quote terminal.
- An options Greeks platform.
- A trade recommendation engine.
- A tax filing product.
- A social leaderboard.

## Naming Principles

1. Prefer names that feel memorable before they feel literal. Tactile trader words are useful, but the final brand can be more evocative if the tagline anchors it.
2. Make the name feel like a working tool, not a vague fintech intelligence brand.
3. Avoid claims that imply live data, auto-trading, advice, broker sync, or AI recommendations.
4. Keep the mobile mark short: 2-4 letters should work in the header.
5. The name should survive both an options-income user and a swing-trade user.
6. Legal, trademark, and domain checks are required before committing.
7. If using Indian/Hindu-inspired language, prefer concepts like purpose, record, target, discipline, insight, and cycles. Avoid using deity names, sacred symbols, or temple-style visuals as decorative branding unless there is a deliberate cultural reason and review.

## Naming Territories

### 0. The Saga

Meaning: the app turns a trader's closed trades, lessons, wins, losses, and recurring patterns into a personal record. It is less literal than tape/book/desk language, but more ownable.

Strengths:

- More memorable than finance-compound names.
- Fits a trader reviewing the story of their performance over time.
- Gives the product a personality without implying live quotes or advice.
- Works well with a compact mobile mark.

Risks:

- Less immediately descriptive; the tagline has to do real work.
- Mythic language can become too decorative if the UI leans into it too hard.
- Requires legal, trademark, and domain checks like every candidate.

Candidate names:

- Edda
- TradeSaga
- SagaBook
- PnLSaga
- CloseSaga

### 0B. The Artha / Lakshya Territory

Meaning: use Indian philosophical and Sanskrit-adjacent language around purpose, value, target, record, and disciplined review. This can carry cultural resonance without making the product feel like it is using sacred imagery as decoration.

Strengths:

- More distinctive than generic fintech naming.
- Connects naturally to the user's trading goal, capital at work, and the meaning behind results.
- Can feel personal and culturally rooted while staying modern.
- Works with the Trader Cockpit if the visual system remains restrained.

Risks:

- Some words are common across Indian startups and funds, so trademark/domain checks matter.
- Some words may be unfamiliar to non-Indian users and need a clear tagline.
- Avoid drifting into religious iconography; the app is a performance tool, not a spiritual product.

Candidate names:

- Artha
- Lakshya
- Lekha
- Drishti
- Sutra
- Niti
- Kosha
- Yatra
- Chakra

Names to handle carefully or avoid:

- Veda: powerful but too sacred/general for a trading analytics app.
- Lakshmi, Kubera, Ganesha, Krishna, Arjuna: recognizable, but using deity/epic figure names for a finance dashboard can feel heavy or commodifying.
- Mantra: widely used in tech/finance and can feel cliche.
- Chakra: relevant to cycles/wheel, but strongly associated with spiritual/wellness branding.

### 1. The Tape

Meaning: a trader's running record. In this app, it is not live market tape; it is your realized-results tape.

Strengths:

- Trader-native and energetic.
- Supports the ticker leaderboard concept naturally.
- Works well with the Trader Cockpit visual direction.

Risks:

- Can imply real-time quote tape if copy is not careful.
- Needs a qualifier in UI: realized tape, trade tape, closed trade tape.

Candidate names:

- TradeTape
- PnLTape
- CloseTape
- The Trade Tape
- ResultTape

### 2. The Book

Strengths:

Risks:

Candidate names:

- CloseBook
- TradeBook

Strengths:

Risks:

Strengths:

Meaning: monthly pace, annual goal progress, consistency, run rate.

Risks:

Candidate names:

### 6. The Desk

Meaning: a compact working surface for trader review.

Candidate names:

## Council Shortlist

### 1. Edda

Tagline: Your trading saga.

Why it works:

- Short, distinctive, and much more ownable than the earlier literal candidates.
- The tagline frames the app as the personal story of a trader's realized results: wins, losses, lessons, pace, and open loops.
- Avoids false claims about live data, trade advice, or broker sync.
- Works nicely with the Trader Cockpit because the cockpit becomes the current chapter, while the ledger/detail views become the saga archive.
- Mobile mark: `E` or `EDDA`.

Watchouts:

- The name is evocative rather than descriptive, so first-run UI and README copy must quickly say `realized trading performance`.
- Avoid overusing Norse/mythic styling. The brand should be modern and restrained, not fantasy themed.
- Needs legal/domain diligence.

### 2. Artha

Tagline: Purpose behind every trade.

Why it works:

- `Artha` carries meanings around purpose, meaning, value, and material prosperity, which maps elegantly to realized performance and capital efficiency.
- It feels culturally rooted without needing religious imagery.
- The name is short, serious, and broad enough for options income, swing trades, tax lots, and goal pace.
- Mobile mark: `A` or `AR`.

Watchouts:

- It is less obviously a trading app without the tagline or supporting copy.
- `Artha` is already used by finance/investment organizations in some markets, so legal/domain checks are especially important.
- Avoid temple, deity, lotus-heavy, or ornamental visual language; use a modern target/ledger mark instead.

### 3. Lakshya

Tagline: Keep your trades on target.

Why it works:

- `Lakshya` means aim/target, which pairs directly with monthly and annual goal pacing.
- It is motivational without implying trade advice.
- Good fit for the cockpit's MTD pace and action queue.
- Mobile mark: `L` or `LAK`.

Watchouts:

- More goal-oriented than ledger-oriented.
- Pronunciation may need a little help for a broad audience.
- Also needs trademark/domain diligence.

### 4. Lekha

Tagline: Your trading record.

Why it works:

- `Lekha` relates to writing, account, or record, making it a very clean fit for a local realized-performance ledger.
- Feels more grounded than mythic.
- Works well for import, review/fix, tax lots, and detail explanations.
- Mobile mark: `L` or `LE`.

Watchouts:

- Calmer and more accounting-like than the Trader Cockpit direction.
- Less emotionally vivid than Edda or Artha.

### 5. TradeTape

Tagline: Your realized trading tape.

Why it works:

- Best match for Option A Trader Cockpit.
- Makes the ticker leaderboard feel native.
- Short, energetic, and easy to say.
- Mobile mark: `TT` or `TAPE`.

Watchouts:

- Must avoid suggesting live quotes. UI copy should say `Realized movers`, `Closed trade tape`, or `Your trade tape`.

### 6. CloseBook

Tagline: Know what closed, what paid, and what needs review.

Why it works:

- Strongest product-truth fit.
- Captures realized P&L, closed events, and review/fix workflows.
- More distinctive than `Ledger` while still trustworthy.
- Mobile mark: `CB`.

Watchouts:

- Slightly less exciting than TradeTape.
- Needs open-position action queue copy to avoid feeling only historical.

### 7. CaptureDesk

Tagline: Track option income, swing gains, and capital at work.

Why it works:

- Active and practical.
- Fits options premium capture while still allowing swing trade results.
- Pairs well with action queue and performance desk language.
- Mobile mark: `CD`.

Watchouts:

- More generic than TradeTape or CloseBook.
- Needs domain/trademark diligence.

### 8. PnLDeck

Tagline: A cockpit for your realized P&L.

Why it works:

- Distinctive and compact.
- `Deck` maps naturally to dashboard cards, cockpit panels, and trader review.
- Keeps the product centered on realized P&L.
- Mobile mark: `PNL` or `DECK`.

Watchouts:

- `PnL` can be visually awkward in a wordmark.
- More inside-baseball, but retail traders will understand it.

### 9. TradePace

Tagline: Stay on pace with your realized trading goals.

Why it works:

- Best fit for goal progress, MTD pace, and annual target storytelling.
- Friendly and motivating.
- Works beyond options-specific workflows.
- Mobile mark: `TP`.

Watchouts:

- Less unique.
- Does not immediately signal the ledger/detail side of the app.

## Names To Avoid

- RealizedEdge: rejected; also feels advisory and generic.
- PositionIQ: rejected; `IQ` feels generic and product-suite-ish.
- TradeDesk: too close to known brand territory.
- OptionIQ, GreeksIQ, WheelBot, AutoWheel: imply scope or automation the product does not have.
- TradeAlert, MarketPulse, LiveTape: imply real-time data.
- WealthBuilder, CompoundWealth: too aspirational for an operational performance tool.
- RobinhoodSync: implies broker integration and brand dependency.

## Brand Personality

Recommended personality: direct, trader-native, confident, and private.

Voice examples:

- `Your realized trading tape`
- `Your trading saga`
- `Purpose behind every trade`
- `Keep your trades on target`
- `Your trading record`
- `The story of what worked`
- `Every close has a chapter`
- `What closed, what paid, what needs review`
- `Top movers by realized P&L`
- `On pace this month`
- `Capital at work`
- `Review the math`

Avoid:

- `Beat the market with AI`
- `Trade smarter instantly`
- `Live portfolio intelligence`
- `Automated options strategy`

## Visual Branding Directions

### A. Tape Mark

Best paired with: TradeTape, PnLTape, CloseTape.

Visual idea:

- Compact horizontal strip with three ticks or cells.
- One cell can turn green/red in leaderboard contexts.
- Mobile mark can be `TT` inside a rounded square.

Tone:

- Energetic, trader-native, strongest match for Option A.

### A2. Edda Mark

Best paired with: Edda.

Visual idea:

- A compact rune-like `E` made from three horizontal ticks and one vertical stem.
- The ticks can subtly echo a tape/ledger without becoming a fantasy mark.
- Mobile mark can be a single `E` in a rounded square.

Tone:

- Distinctive, story-driven, and modern. Keep it restrained so `saga` feels like performance history, not decoration.

### A3. Artha Mark

Best paired with: Artha, Lakshya, Lekha.

Visual idea:

- A modern target/ledger hybrid: one vertical stem, one closing tick, and a small center point.
- For `Artha`, the mark can imply meaning/value coming into focus.
- For `Lakshya`, the same system can lean target-like.
- For `Lekha`, the same system can lean ledger-line-like.

Tone:

- Culturally rooted but not ornamental. Avoid religious motifs, deity imagery, mandalas, or temple silhouettes.

### B. Book Mark

Best paired with: CloseBook, TradeBook, TradeLedger.

Visual idea:

- Minimal open-book or ledger-line symbol, not literal pages.
- Two vertical strokes plus a closing tick can imply closed trades.
- Mobile mark can be `CB`.

Tone:

- Trustworthy, local-first, review-oriented.

### C. Capture Mark

Best paired with: CaptureDesk, PremiumCapture, CaptureBook.

Visual idea:

- A small bracket or frame around a tick mark.
- Suggests capturing outcomes without looking like a camera app.
- Mobile mark can be `CD` or a bracketed check.

Tone:

- Active, practical, options-income friendly.

### D. Deck Mark

Best paired with: PnLDeck.

Visual idea:

- Stacked panels or cards with a rising/loss tick.
- Works naturally with dashboard cards and cockpit layout.
- Mobile mark can be a stacked-card icon.

Tone:

- Modern, UI-native, a little more playful.

## Recommended Direction

The council recommendation is to explore `Edda` and `Artha` as the two expressive leads, then compare them against the strongest literal alternatives before renaming production code:

1. `Edda` for the most distinctive and ownable brand.
2. `Artha` for the strongest Indian/Hindu-inspired direction without leaning on sacred iconography.
3. `TradeTape` for the most explicit Trader Cockpit brand.
4. `CloseBook` for the most honest and durable product-truth brand.

A third alternate, `CaptureDesk`, is worth keeping if the app should lean harder into options income and action workflows.

## Next Mockup Step

Create or review the same Trader Cockpit header and mobile shell in five brand skins:

1. Edda: rune/tick mark, saga language, distinctive performance-history feel.
2. Artha: target/ledger mark, purpose/value language, Indian-inspired but restrained.
3. TradeTape: tape-strip mark, realized ticker language, high-energy cockpit.
4. CloseBook: ledger-line mark, closed-event language, trust/accounting feel.
5. CaptureDesk: bracket/check mark, premium/action language, income workflow feel.

No production rename should happen until the preferred name passes basic legal/domain checks and feels right in the UI.
